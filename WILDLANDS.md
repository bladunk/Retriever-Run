# Wildlands engineering notes

## A. Prototype audit and plan

The prototype ships Three.js 0.180 and Vite 7 with no external game art, audio, or runtime services. GitHub Actions builds PRs and deploys the relative-path Vite output on main. Keep that architecture and the existing Pages URL.

The prototype binds Pointer Events and keyboard input to a shared action handler, pauses hidden tabs, and persists a browser-local record. Keep those paths. The scene currently requires WebGL 2 and caps device pixel ratio. Six merged terrain tiles recycle along a straight Z axis. Obstacles use a single center-plane crossing and random rows with at least one clear lane, but the generator has no timing proof and difficulty depends only on distance. Character motion uses rigid ellipsoids and a single rotation per leg. Audio is synthesized one-shots. There is no automated test entry point.

1. Keep simulation independent from rendering. Add a seeded course, time-based progression, a shared lane model and a conservative route validator. Cover these with Node tests.
2. Replace the player with a modular anatomical model and articulated stride, jump, slide and landing poses. Retain a procedural fallback if an appropriate licensed rig cannot be verified.
3. Establish a continuous trail coordinate shared by the camera, hazards, pickups and mesh generation. Recycle visible tiles, generate habitat dressing by region, and verify curves and seams.
4. Add timed gates and moving lane hazards within the validator's safe route. Keep the opening learnable and ramp the course through the three-minute mark.
5. Add pooled flock models and a gradual weather director with low and high render budgets.
6. Extend the record format compatibly, add retrieve and cosmetic rewards, layer audio, and restyle and verify every screen. Test the production build at a project subpath and capture the game in a mobile landscape viewport.

## B. Labrador and animation

`src/dog.js` owns the player model and exposes a small pose and appearance interface. The torso and otter tail use cross-section meshes, and separate pivots articulate the ears, neck, shoulders, hips, knees, and feet. Two-link leg solving drives a planted stance and a lifted swing. Leap, landing compression, lean, and slide blend over the locomotion pose. This is an original stylized-anatomical model. Foot contact is approximate and the coat uses a subtle generated surface grain.

FeralMotion's [rigged Labrador](https://feralmotion.itch.io/labrador) was a close fit visually but its license restricts redistribution of the asset files and the source package is 148 MB. The [CC0 Labrador listing](https://3dassets.dev/assets/pets-and-companions-hd-labrador-retriever-927d54b7) identifies its mesh as a static posed study. Neither is bundled. The dog, terrain, wildlife, prop geometry, effects, and audio in Wildlands are generated by project source. There are no third-party asset files. Three.js and Vite retain their upstream MIT licenses.

## C. Curved trail and habitats

`src/trail.js` provides a continuous centerline, analytic heading, and perpendicular lane space. Tiles are sampled against that same centerline, and the follow camera, dog, pickups, moving debris, and static hazards use the course position and heading. Terrain uses continuous elevation at habitat transitions. The display and simulation use longitudinal course meters. The traveled world-space arc is slightly longer around bends.

The five recurring habitats use separate ground palettes and dressing, with flooded snags, potholes, autumn woodland, muddy banks, cattail stands, intermittent bridges, shallow crossings, camouflaged blinds, and noninteractive decoy spreads. Geometry is recycled across seven visible tile slots. Track curvature and the minimap-free camera provide route context.

## D. Difficulty and validation

The tuning table in `src/runner.js` interpolates difficulty from 2 at the start to 9.5 at 180 seconds, raising the speed from 11 to 26.9 course meters per second. Row spacing in seconds tightens, double blocks grow more common, and bounded moving debris and wide slide gates join the course. Each run generates a seeded sequence.

The validator certifies one lane route per row with a 380 ms reaction allowance, lane settling, a crossing interval, and jump/slide recovery margins. Adjacent drift is bounded outside the protected route. When a row fails validation the generator substitutes a clear row. This verifies that a route exists from an earlier certified lane. Choosing a different route can require different inputs.

## E. Wildlife and weather

The weather director blends eight conditions with a 12-second transition, and conditions hold long enough to affect a stretch of the run. Weather changes fog, moving cloud cover, lighting, wet-ground roughness, wind, rain, snow, and sleet. Fog and precipitation are bounded so the near lane remains visible. Flocks use reusable instanced body and wing geometry with species-specific motion and silhouettes. The species include Canada geese, mallards, low-flying teal, and flushing pheasants.

## F. Rewards, UI, audio, and performance

The original browser record key still loads old scores and distances. Runs add treat combos, retrieve rewards, cosmetic unlocks, and milestones. Retrieve pickups rise and spin with an audio cue while player input stays active. The outfitter and settings share the persisted profile. Web Audio generates weather beds, surface-dependent footsteps, landing and splash effects, birds, pickups, and an optional music bed. Playback uses a start, resume, or settings gesture to unlock Web Audio.

The scene caps pixel ratio and framebuffer dimensions, merges static terrain by tile, pools pickup and obstacle models, and instances reeds and birds. Precipitation buffers are reused. Auto quality drops to a smaller framebuffer and lighter vegetation, flock, and precipitation budgets after sustained slow frames. Explicit High and Battery saver overrides are available. Rendering stops on static pause and report screens until an update is needed. The Vite build splits the cacheable Three.js engine from game code. There are no model or texture downloads to decompress.

`npm test` exercises physics, timing, weather, profiles, track continuity, and 80 independently seeded routes through 253 seconds without power-ups. Production browser smoke uses an iPhone-sized touch viewport and a static server at `/Retriever-Run/`. Native Safari frame rate, battery life, and audio have not been measured on a physical iPhone.
