# Retriever Run player-first charter

Retriever Run is free, ad-free and free of purchases. Each feature should make the game more enjoyable, immersive, rewarding, accessible, or reliable. A run should be easy to start, difficult to master, and worth exploring.

## Permanent owner commitments

1. No advertisements.
2. No pay-to-win mechanics.
3. No loot boxes.
4. No mandatory daily challenges.
5. No login streak penalties.
6. No energy limits or restricted lives.
7. No forced account creation.
8. No intrusive notifications.
9. No artificial scarcity or timed purchase pressure.
10. No unnecessary grinding.
11. No unavoidable deaths.
12. No sacrificing visual clarity to inflate FPS.
13. No unnecessary paid infrastructure.

## How we apply the charter

- Keep advertisements, microtransactions, pay-to-win mechanics, paid loot boxes, energy systems, and limited lives out of the game.
- Keep daily or weekly challenges, logins, social sharing, and notifications optional. Avoid login streaks, inactivity penalties, forced notification permission requests, and unnecessary registration.
- Use the time a genuine operation takes. Avoid artificial waiting periods, fake loading progress, manipulative countdowns, and mandatory reward-claiming chores.
- Build progression around meaningful play. Avoid arbitrary barriers, deliberately frustrating interactions, unavoidable deaths, and unnecessary interruptions during hunts.
- Collect the minimum information needed to operate features players choose. Player information is never sold. Solo progression stays available without an online account.
- Give players clear controls for sound, motion, haptics where supported, and graphics. Preserve readable hazards and identical mechanics at every graphics setting.

Replayability comes from skill, discovery, competition, environmental variation, local progress, and desirable cosmetic rewards. Prioritize the quality and variety of a feature over the number of entries it adds. Review this charter before adding a new mechanic, permission prompt, or progression gate.

## Obstacle readability

- Judge every lethal obstacle from the moving gameplay camera on a small portrait screen. Include Battery Saver, Balanced and High, Auto transitions, early and fast courses, and difficult fog, rain, frost and shadow.
- The silhouette must show which lane is occupied and whether the escape is a jump, a slide or a lane change. A player must be able to recognize it before the last safe input, with the existing course reaction allowance and maneuver setup time available.
- Use the collision footprint to audit what the player sees. Let weathered or irregular art soften edges while preserving the dangerous area and the clearance under trunks, branches and equipment. Keep decorative wet ground distinguishable from dangerous water.
- Keep close grass, rocks, scenery, shadows and distance transitions from hiding the hazard. Prefer placement, ground conformity, materials and contrast to extra HUD alerts or changes to the signed course.
- Keep one matched running-camera capture and the corresponding input-window check with any terrain or hazard review. Close-ups and debug hulls help explain a defect. The moving view and supported physical devices establish player readability.
