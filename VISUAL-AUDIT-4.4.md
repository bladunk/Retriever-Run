# Retriever Run 4.4 · Operation Wild Country

## 1. Executive assessment

**Improve the existing Labrador. Spend $0 in Phase A.** The shipped dog is recognizably Labrador, uses one 9,967-triangle skin, and already survives the game’s jump, low pose, and cosmetic regressions. Its worst close-up defect is fixable in the current rig. The fitted vest mask paints parts of the head, mouth, and front legs in the released build. A replacement would inherit the work of fitting gear, retargeting motion, and matching the tested collision envelope.

**Put the first discretionary asset dollars into wetland silhouettes.** The opening run submits roughly 240,000–246,000 triangles in 47–48 median draws. The guided route peaked at 278,972 submitted triangles during the woodland and creek transition. Most of that cost lives in baked terrain and scenery. Low reduces reed density, shader work, and resolution while keeping the same static terrain geometry. Separating the near banks from distant scenery makes room for better reeds and a few distinctive wetland trees. If an approved pack passes the specifications below, reserve **$15–35 USD for one foliage kit** and **$15–45 for one hero waterfowl asset** only if the in-house bird redesign falls short. The conditional ceiling is **$80 USD** for licenses. Keep the current obstacles and budget time to improve their materials and readability.

The audit uses Retriever Run 4.3 on main at `ed1b456`, the merge of PR #10. `npm run build` produced the statically deployable game, and `npm test` passed all 78 tests. All captures and measurements use the shipped art and local builds. The test host ran Linux Chromium and WebKit. Physical iPhone performance remains unmeasured. ## 2. Current visual asset inventory

### Labrador and cosmetics

The binary at `src/assets/labrador.glb` is **292,936 bytes** on disk. Its single indexed triangle primitive has **29,901 indices, 9,967 triangles, 6,427 indexed vertices, one material, and one skin**. The binary has already been triangulated, so original quad topology cannot be inferred. The earlier 3.0 notes list 9,969 triangles. The current count comes directly from the shipped index accessor. The GLB uses Meshopt compression and quantized attributes. The 61 scene nodes include 54 skin joints, with 47 joints receiving nonzero weights. Four influences are available per vertex. The longest bounds of the bind mesh after game scaling are about **0.73 × 1.51 × 2.96 game units** across width, height, and nose-to-tail length. The rig uses one embedded **768 × 768 JPEG albedo** of 82,177 bytes. Three.js supplies the physically lit material at roughness .90 and metalness 0. The file carries no fur normal, dedicated roughness, eye gloss, or separate nose material. Coat variants tint the existing image. The cream shader lightens selected warm pixels. Shoulder modeling, the recognizably broad Lab head, and the tail work from the chase camera. In the portrait close-ups, the angular muzzle-to-cheek transition, shadowed eyes, thick uniform ear surfaces, partially fused toe silhouette, and smooth coat read at lower fidelity than the form. The waist and tail become noticeably lean in some poses. Preserve the athletic working-dog proportions while making the broad skull, short dense coat, and thick tail base read more clearly. [AKC Labrador breed standard](https://cdn.akc.org/LabradorRetriever.pdf)

**Confirmed material defect.** The vest turns into a pale or orange patch over the forequarters, and part of the muzzle, ears, and forelegs picks up the same color. The shader derives its mask from the raw quantized mesh coordinates as `position.xzy`. A CPU probe using the same saddle/strap boundaries found 3,129 of 6,427 rest vertices inside the approximate 50% mask. Of those, 2,374 were dominated by head, neck, ear, or mouth weights, and 528 by front limb weights. The probe counts vertices and does not measure rendered area. The controlled gear capture shows the visible spill. Author a torso-local mask in verified non-overlapping UVs or an explicit vertex attribute, with separate binding and strap detail. Keep it inside the existing skin material and exclude muzzle, ears, and lower limbs by test. ## 3. Technical performance measurements

The prior 4.3 QA report observed one GLB request and 49 draw calls in one early frame across 4.2, 4.3 Classic, and a geared 4.3 dog. This audit measured several frames and included the driver-visible vertex/index allocation sizes. See the [measurement appendix](docs/visual-audit-4.4/MEASUREMENTS.md) for the route samples, startup results, methods, and limits. | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | 358 × 776 | 48 | 240,256 | 18.95 MiB |
| Balanced | 475 × 1,029 | 47 | 242,940 | 18.95 MiB |
| High | 612 × 1,325 | 48 | 246,008 | 18.95 MiB |
| Kennel, 390 × 844 CSS at test DPR 1 | 346 × 233 | 5 | 10,459 Classic / 10,467 buckle and vest | 18.53 MiB total retained scene allocations |

Triangles are submissions, including triangles hidden by rasterization, fog, or other geometry. Flock and reed counts include instances. The Kennel retains the world buffers for return to play. It draws the dog, its accessories, the blob shadow, and the floor. The skinned dog plus Classic collar and tag submits 10,455 triangles in three draws before the shadow. Buckle configuration is 10,463 in three, with the vest adding no geometry. The legacy bandana adds one draw.

- **Asset transfer.** The freshly built `dist` occupied **1,998,464 bytes** before HTTP compression, including audio, optional SDK, worker, and all build artifacts. The four ground/timber WebPs total **190,364 bytes**, and the GLB is **292,936 bytes**. The visuals accounted for 483,300 bytes of binary asset bodies, separate from code. Texture file sizes and decoded GPU residency are different measurements.
- **Memory.** Explicit current dog vertex/index buffers total **239,758 bytes** (234 KiB) before accessories and bone data. The 16 × 16 RGBA32F bone texture is 4 KiB. With conventional RGBA8 allocations and full mip chains, the coat is about 3.00 MiB, the two 512px base color maps 2.67 MiB together and the two 256px packed maps 0.67 MiB together. Including the blob shadow, fallback texture and bone data yields an estimated **6.37 MiB** of art textures. Current game textures can be retained even if a preset does less sampling. The framebuffer is separate. A 4-sample RGBA8/depth illustration with a resolved color surface gives roughly **9.5 / 16.8 / 27.8 MiB** for the measured Low/Balanced/High buffer sizes. Driver tiling, depth layout, compositor surfaces and transient buffers vary.
- **Startup and animation.** 4.3’s prior 390 × 844 software samples had a 1.83 s median to a prepared trail and visible skin, with a 1.59–3.29 s range. Fresh loopback probes in this audit took 2.37–6.59 s across the three preset samples under mixed host load. An isolated Node measurement of 3,000 pose calls per state observed p50/p95 of **0.071/0.140 ms idle**, **0.067/0.139 ms gallop**, **0.071/0.148 ms jump**, and **0.125/0.213 ms full slide**. It measures JavaScript mixer, bones and floor support only. It excludes GPU skinning, asset loading, and the game loop.
- **Frame times.** Early software-WebGL samples were short, with 8–10 rendered frames per preset. Median RAF intervals were roughly 683–817 ms in the concurrent audit, while median measured frame callback work was 4–5 ms. Browser scheduling and software rasterization dominate this comparison. The numbers establish submission and allocation pressure and say little about phone GPU throughput. [Exact frame and buffer results](docs/visual-audit-4.4/MEASUREMENTS.md)

## 4. Labrador replacement feasibility

**KEEP the model. IMPROVE WITH CODE and targeted art edits.** Fix the vest mask first, then add closer-range separation for the eye, nose, leather and fur. A restrained roughness mask, subtle short-coat detail, better silhouette shading and two well-tested camera angles for the Kennel will do more than increasing polygon count. If a small eye highlight or normal edit is added, attach it to the existing texture budget and test every coat tint. Avoid layered hair cards, transparent fur, and new per-frame physics for accessories.

Any eventual replacement has to win a side-by-side test under the game’s lighting in both the title/Kennel and chase camera. Require an indexed single skinned primitive, a matching adult working Lab shape, known local up/forward axes, four influences per vertex, and a named or explicitly mapped neck and paw chain. Target **10–12k triangles, hard cap 15k**, ≤60 skin joints, one material, a **768px or 1,024px** compressed coat atlas, and ≤400 kB for the compressed runtime scene including necessary clips. Require source geometry, preserved UVs and weights, plus in-place Idle and Gallop, a root-motion-stripped Jump, and a low pass that fits both normal and wide branch solids. No required engine plugins, shader graphs, or audio license dependencies.

Replacement work includes adapting head and body pivots, matching the shoulder and paw shapes, source retarget time, fitting the collar and vest for every legacy cosmetic, renaming/indexing animation clips, and retesting muzzle, paw, and body impacts on curves across slow and max speed. Confirm exact silhouette against the existing collision overlays before negotiating any visual envelope change. The 4.4 scope freezes collision and traversal. Use the fallback procedural dog only as a loading or failure fallback.

**FeralMotion’s supplied Labrador is a reference and a deferred replacement.** The uploaded GLB rendered in a private Three.js comparison and shows clearer eye, jaw, and paw definition under the same lighting. The actual web exports are **72.2k triangles and 12.4–14.5 MB** before HTTP compression. The standalone GLB has two material primitives, 47 joints and four 2K maps. The jump tracks translate forward, the clip set has no compatible low slide, and the game’s existing neck and paw attachments need a new bone mapping. The free license covers game use and restricts redistribution of the asset files themselves. Confirm permission for the exact public repository and browser delivery before shipping. Its measured cost and integration work keep the recommendation at **DEFER**. Use the close-up as a reference for refining the current dog. ## 5. Ranked visual weaknesses and highest-impact changes

1. **Thin ground cover under repetitive crowns.** Make the first 20–35 m feel occupied by actual marsh vegetation. Add distinguishable cattail, sedge and rush silhouettes, small irregular bank clumps, and a few prominent willow, alder and snag shapes. Shift distant trees to cheap silhouettes. This improves the widest portion of the phone view while addressing the main geometry load.
2. **The dog’s cosmetic finish breaks its anatomy.** Remove the measured vest spill, put a real fabric boundary on the torso, and improve nose, eye, ear and fur definition at the two close camera distances. Preserve the healthy broad head and stance. This improves the title and Kennel where the Labrador sells the game.
3. **Water and banks have weak depth cues.** Add a narrow shallow-water transition, flow around the actual crossing, stems and debris at the shoreline, and slightly stronger contact contrast on land. Keep fog as a separation tool and give each habitat a distinct value range. A second reflection pass has poor value relative to these changes.
4. **Playable timber looks clean and interchangeable.** Make fallen bark, broken fence rails, a snag and overhead branch obviously different at the same value range. Create minute irregularity, end grain, damp material, and constrained projected contact shading. Ask players to identify jump/duck/steer from a 25–35 m capture in daylight and rain before approval.
5. **Near wildlife looks hinged.** Make the actionable goose’s tapered neck, cheek patch, wing flex, and primary feather silhouette readable from the chase camera. Keep ambient flocks economical. Evaluate the one hero model against the current attack path and warning timing, then decide whether an external bird saves enough work.

## 8. Recommended free improvements

- **Correct the apparel mask on the existing mesh.** Author one torso mask and test the highest XP coat/vest combinations, Classic, and legacy bandana. Reuse shading and existing lights. A painted seam, muted fold shading, and independent roughness can give depth without a second mesh.
- **Separate the ground from heavy static dressing.** Keep the base grid indexed and put dense vegetation in near/far instanced groups. The current 3,360-triangle base grid alone becomes 10,080 non-indexed vertices. At the current 30 bytes per expanded vertex, preserving 1,769 vertices and a 16-bit index would reduce that grid’s attributes/indices by about **229 kB per tile** before counting scenery. Quantize shared leaf and rock meshes. Start testing near 0–35 m, mid 35–90 m, and far 90–150 m ranges with hysteresis. Use only a few tile-side batches and measure the draw increase against geometry saved.
- **Separate the shore materials cheaply.** Blend bank/water by the existing height and wet data, add low-frequency shore color breakup, and place shallow wave flecks where the creek crosses. Incremental terrain geometry uses existing collision paths. Keep puddle and stream opacity, width, and visible depth clear under fog.
- **Ground hazards, snags, and the dog.** Borrow the existing blob-shadow approach for selected close objects or put a cheap contact term in their shared materials. Add a UV scale for bark and end grain, and make low logs and high gates distinct at the same pixel sizes. Compare sunrise, full fog and heavy rain.
- **Refine gear, bird silhouettes, and mobile hierarchy.** Make collar hardware and canvas distinguishable with the existing gear states. Block out a stronger wing/neck silhouette using the current flock batching. Compact the Lodge masthead, keep the preview visible, increase HUD labels, and leave room below the Field Report action tray.

## 9. Mobile performance risks

1. **Tile build and upload cost.** Baked tiles contain roughly 2.02–3.36 MB of transferable data per tile in the 30-seed-index sample. At 28.7 course m/s the camera can approach a new 30 m tile every 1.05 s. Worker generation limits blocking, but WebGL buffer upload, CPU copies and shader compilation still run at awkward times. Preserve worker transfer, recycling, disposal, watchdog and pause gating.
2. **Unchanged static geometry across presets.** Reducing DPR and reed counts cannot remove distant baked trees and banks. Fog blends pixels but still incurs submissions. Split only where bounds materially improve culling and cap extra draws. Keep the original path geometry, warning leads, and the seeded generator.
3. **Fill cost and framebuffer memory.** High drives nearly 2.92× Low’s pixels at the tested viewport. MSAA, procedural sky, water and overlapping transparent foliage or particles can increase work disproportionate to triangle count. Test with rain and active geese. Do not use large blended vegetation cards, real-time reflections, dynamic shadow maps, or a second Kennel renderer for the first pass.
4. **Safari formats, thermal limits, and background recovery.** Compressed texture fallbacks, texture upload, context loss/restoration, tab eviction, and thermal throttling need physical tests. WPE’s reported renderer string is not evidence of an Apple device. A proposed device pair is a recent A16-or-newer iPhone for the 60 FPS goal and an A13-class device such as iPhone 11 for 30 FPS, subject to the app’s supported iOS version. Record exact iOS/Safari versions and battery/thermal conditions.
5. **Art and collision drift.** Gear, feet and wings must stay visibly aligned with the swept hit areas. A subtle material shader can change perceived hazard size even without changing geometry. Retest jump/slide timing, stream boundaries, diver path and off-lane plants against the collision diagnostics, at both pace limits.
